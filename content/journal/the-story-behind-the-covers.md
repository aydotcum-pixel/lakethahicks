## Book One

I was sleeping one day and the Lord spoke to me about the lotus flower. I had never heard of this flower and decided to look it up. When I discovered the lotus flower I learned that it symbolized rebirth, resilience, and a spiritual awakening. I kept this in mind.

When I began to design the cover for book one, I thought what better way to represent the main character than that of a flower. I kept the lotus flower in mind because it grows in muddy water and blooms above the surface. I collaborated with my youngest sibling, Lola, who drew the design for the cover. Together we brainstormed and came up with the meaning behind the photo.

Tamela the main character is represented by a flower. When writing the book I knew the Lord wanted every woman to see themselves in Tamela which is why she is not defined by physical features but by a flower in bloom. On book one you can see her rising from the murky waters, much like that of the lotus flower to bloom into the woman she was always meant to be.

My mom’s favorite color was yellow so we thought that we would pay homage to her by having the flower be yellow.

The briars represent all of the things that have her bound from pain, regret, unforgiveness, doubt, lack of faith, unhealthy relationships, and a whole slew of things the main character Tamela faced.

The butterflies were also a symbol of transformation and rebirth. They represent the different stages of life that take place before we emerge into the beauty that the world sees. Tamela like the butterfly had an emotional and spiritual metamorphosis, much like the physical metamorphosis of a butterfly.

## Book Two

She was no longer bound by some of the things that held her in book one, but there was still work to be done so some vines still held her. She began to flourish which is why she is in full bloom. The growth in her life can be seen by others. She is in the process of rebirth, beginning anew. The silhouette of who she is, is coming into full bloom.
